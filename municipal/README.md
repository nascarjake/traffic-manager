# MUNICIPAL — Traffic Control Simulator

A playable browser-based WebGL traffic game with a late-1990s municipal workstation interface. JavaScript modules and locally bundled Three.js; no runtime services or credentials required. A pinned esbuild development dependency produces the static release.

## Run

Node 20+ and Python 3 are sufficient:

```sh
cd municipal
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open http://127.0.0.1:5173. `npm run build` bundles, tree-shakes and minifies the game into content-hashed ESM chunks in a self-contained static `dist/` for any static host. Internet is needed only for finding/importing additional districts and opening source links; the bundled Wicker Park scene and simulation work without external network requests.

## Play

The game starts in Watch view, with no operations panel or cabinet. Click a junction marker, signal head or sign to open only its docked roadside cabinet. Its hardware monitor opens automatically; ×, Escape or V closes it and keeps the city running. Show controls exposes the operations menu without automatically opening a cabinet. Each incoming compass approach shows left/through/right permission and its waiting count. The Control tab follows the active control type. Stop and priority junctions use a field inspection sheet with actual approach signs (unsigned priority is explicitly marked NO SIGN), gap rules, queues, and pedestrians; they have no signal heads, WALK indicators, timing program or quick retiming controls. Signal junctions open a textured metal enclosure with hinges, a screwed nameplate, illuminated field outputs, a controller LCD, load-switch LEDs and detector calls. Monitor and Program are physical mode keys; Program shows editable street/movement cards, timing sliders, protected-arrow switches and pedestrian timing. The preview is an edit buffer, distinct from live outputs. Quick controls add five seconds to the current street’s through stage or balance through stages at 25 seconds. The cabinet’s live output monitor shows red/yellow/green for each incoming compass direction, detector calls, queues and phase elapsed time. Adjust fixed-time phases or switch to detector-actuated control, and write the timing program. Protected left arrows have separate green/yellow/all-red stages; through and right-turn traffic wait while a protected arrow runs. Without protection, left-turners yield to opposing traffic on circular green. An exclusive pedestrian stage admits people on WALK, flashes DON’T WALK during calculated clearance, and holds all vehicles red until every crossing clears. The cabinet shows live arrows, walk indications, countdowns, detector calls and waiting counts. Request crossing adds a pedestrian call. Clearances are preserved. Use Road works to convert signal/stop control, add lanes, and install a left-turn bay within a $50,000 budget. Test your plan compares two seeded four-minute runs and awards the assignment at 20% lower delay with no fewer completed vehicle trips or pedestrian crossings. Click a moving car or open Vehicles to see its named origin, destination, purpose, remaining distance and route. Locate vehicle pans to it. A selected junction receives a pulsing amber target and world-anchored ID. Its incoming lanes show stronger live-permission bands, a stop-line output and the leading car’s movement arrow. Each approach has transparent in-world left/through/right outputs and a live WAIT count; amber strips show the actual stopped queue footprint. Cyan trails and left/right counters show cars already in a turn, using the original incoming approach even after the car crosses the logical edge boundary. Other lane glyphs, street labels and vehicle cues recede while the junction is selected. No cabinet or additional window opens for Tab/IJKL selection. Signal permission does not promise immediate admission: vehicles still yield to conflicts and wait for downstream space. The lane-right-of-way layer hides the road readouts while retaining the selection target. These cues are original generated geometry and canvas lettering; no external assets are added. The default Traffic lens draws screen-scaled vehicle outlines and heading/turn symbols: white moving, cyan turning, amber waiting. Waiting turners keep their turn symbol. The lens can be disabled without hiding actual vehicles. Live lane arrows show green permission, amber yield/clearance and red hold; selecting a junction expands permitted connector paths. Focus junction moves the camera above the chosen intersection, centering it in the unobstructed map. In the Test inspector tab, Send traffic wave launches a synthetic 10-, 30-, 60- or 120-car practice run with mixed, left, through or right movements. Traffic uses normal safe entry, following, signal, yielding and connector conflict rules. Each wave starts from empty at the same clock; demand and ambient pedestrian generation are disabled, but Request crossing can add a pedestrian call. Waves last at most 180 simulation seconds and stop early when all cars clear; silver requires 80% cleared, gold requires every car within 120 seconds. Replay compares cleared cars and mean delay with the last completed wave for the same junction, mix and count. Traffic mix/count lock during a drill; return to live district to choose another. The original district simulation, pause state and speed are restored on return; signal and road edits are retained. The traffic-load slider adjusts future arrivals (busy commute defaults to 1.50×), retaining waiting requests. Installing/removing signals waits for the controller’s safe clearance before the inspector and roadside equipment change together. Signal removal also removes obsolete poles/heads rather than leaving dark signal models next to stop signs. The canvas has no floating control panels. Map tools sit in a reserved toolbar; signal readouts, traffic legend and drill scores live outside the map. Watch view (V) hides the side panels and command/menu bars without resetting the simulation. WASD pans the map, QE rotates, ZX zooms, and Shift accelerates camera motion. Tab/Shift+Tab cycles a stable nearest-first intersection list; IJKL selects up/left/down/right relative to the camera and flies there without opening the cabinet. Enter opens the selected cabinet; Shift+Enter applies a timing program. F selects nearest. M opens the current district pin/width editor, with Find a different district available. V toggles the controls; Escape closes the cabinet and returns keyboard focus to the map. 1/2/3 selects 1×/3×/10× time speed. 4/5/6/7 sets quiet/normal/busy/rush traffic: 0.5×/1×/1.5×/2.5× future arrivals and 10/30/60/120-car test waves. Changing level during a drill restarts the wave at the current playback speed and updates the background district’s arrival multiplier. Space or 0 pauses; T opens Test and dispatches/replays a wave; R runs a comparison. H or ? opens the keyboard guide. Camera input works while paused, clears on focus loss and respects normal typing and Tab navigation in controls/dialogs. Drag orbits, right-drag pans, and scroll zooms. PLAN switches to overhead view.

District manager supports explicit place searches through Nominatim, coordinate entry, a map preview of a 900 m, 1.8 km or 2.7 km square (0.81, 3.24 or 7.29 km²), and tiled OSM geometry imports with exact road clipping at the selected boundary. The interactive Leaflet preview lets you drag the pin, click to place it, use the current map center, enter exact coordinates, or nudge the focused pin with arrow keys. The control rectangle follows the pin; imports use that final center and selected width. ZIP-code results are only initial search locations. If a public service fails, the current district remains intact. Save plan stores the district, plans, imported counts, and road edits on the current browser; IndexedDB accommodates large map extracts; browser storage is not cross-device sync.

## Location startup

A small independent module requests a fresh browser fix (`maximumAge: 0`, `enableHighAccuracy: false`, 15-second acquisition timeout) while the WebGL engine downloads, before any demo scene is constructed. Startup stays on “Locating your district” / map progress and opens the nearby scene directly. Use saved / demo district cancels the automatic load immediately. Permission or service failures also use the saved plan or demo. Two OSM tiles load concurrently, with deterministic merging and concurrent Chicago observation retrieval. The successful nearby path does not download or build the bundled Chicago scene, and the road graph is built once rather than validated and rebuilt.

After a successful import, one nearby map is cached locally in IndexedDB, separately from the player's saved plan. Every load still obtains a fresh position before reusing that map; it can be reused for up to 24 hours when the fix is within 50 m of its stored center. Small provider jitter retains that district's original center/boundary and avoids another download or camera jump. A move beyond 50 m or an expired cache imports a new 1.8 km square centered on the fresh fix. Storage failures are cache misses, not startup failures. Permission-change events retry after a grant where supported; Near me provides an explicit retry. A manual district choice cancels late automatic loads. Auto location does not overwrite the saved plan; District → Resume saved district reopens it. No continuous location tracking or location telemetry is implemented. The district bounds go to OpenStreetMap for geometry, and to Chicago’s public data service for nearby counts within Chicago. An embedding page must allow geolocation; otherwise Open for location launches the standalone game. HTTPS/browser permission and provider availability still apply. The browser/provider still controls fix latency; this avoids the high-accuracy preference, which can increase response time ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Geolocation/getCurrentPosition)).

## Data and fidelity

The bundled Wicker Park extract includes 2,299 building footprints and a connected road graph simplified into 130 road links and 64 branching junctions within the extract boundary. Original OSM IDs, names, one-way access, and relevant tags remain in the extract. Nearby branching nodes connected within 23 m are contracted into a single control area, including the North/Damen/Milwaukee junction. A named street and its opposing approach share a phase. Mapped signal, stop and yield nodes survive degree-two road compression. Approach signs are associated with their nearby junction along the road and honor original OSM way direction; central `stop=all` means all-way stop. Signals are never invented from road class. Missing controls use explicitly labeled modeled all-way stop or priority/yield defaults; a single approach stop does not become an all-way stop. OSM supplies control type, not live signal colors or timing plans. Heights without mapped values are procedural estimates.

Chicago's Average Daily Traffic Counts (`gc7y-n4xa`) supply 80 nearby dated observations. Each link can match a same-name count within 450 m. These are dated daily counts, not live arrival rates or turning-movement measurements. Each inbound boundary gateway has a separate exponential-headway request stream. Hourly demand uses modeled shares of 8.5% morning, 9.5% evening and 5% midday, with a modeled directional share of 52% for two-way roads or 95% for one-way roads and a bound of 2,340 requested vehicles/hour per imported lane. Where no count matches, road-class daily estimates range from 1,000 for residential roads to 60,000 for motorways. Highway ramps and arterials therefore request far more traffic than neighborhood boundaries. These assumptions are exposed in the Vehicles arrival-stream list and scaled by the traffic-load setting. They are scenario inputs, not calibrated local flow predictions. CSV imports are labeled unverified.

Local departures use mapped building/amenity activity (homes, workplaces, shops, schools and leisure), bounded floor-area weights and period-specific departure frequencies. Destinations are weighted by trip purpose: morning home departures favor work and school; evening workplace trips favor homes; shopping/leisure trips use their mapped activity locations. Trips may enter, leave, pass through or remain in the district. Every valid request receives named origin/destination metadata and a directed route before admission. Local frontage points lie along public streets at least 24 m from junctions; utility roofs/sheds are omitted, and motorways/trunks never receive local driveways. Frontage points approximate access, not surveyed entrances. Incoming/outgoing gateway permissions respect one-way links. Unroutable destination pairs are resampled. Through-highway components are retained even if their ramps fall outside the district; requests stay within reachable components.

Turn classification samples road alignment 35 m beyond a junction (bounded to 45% of short links), avoiding spurious turns from short kinks at merged OSM nodes. Routes minimize modeled free-flow travel time and reject movements unavailable in imported `turn:lanes` tags. Approach-specific tags retain original OSM way orientation through compression. A dedicated left bay reserves the inner lane; otherwise shared-lane defaults apply where tags are missing. Asymmetric lane geometry and turn-restriction relations are not fully modeled. Driving uses bounded acceleration/braking and an IDM-style following rule, safe adjacent-lane reservations, and explicit cubic junction connectors traversed by arc length. Cars steer continuously across the road-index boundary and enter an appropriate exit lane. Actual connector conflicts block entry; nonconflicting movements can proceed together, with following headways on a shared connector. Downstream space prevents entering a blocked box. Turn indicators and brake lights follow driving state. Vehicles finish at their assigned frontage or boundary rather than disappearing at arbitrary junctions.

Actuated controls implement minimum green, passage gap, gap-out, opposing-call maximum timers, max-out, and resting green. Protected arrows and pedestrian service retain their safety clearances. Fixed controllers support offsets; occupied junctions/crosswalks can extend clearance. Vehicle yellow is 3 s and all-red is at least 2 s. These are teaching defaults, not site-specific engineered intervals. The same movement-permission rules drive the map overlay and vehicle admission; green permission still requires conflict-free space before a car enters.

The matched comparison starts both simulations from empty at the same clock, with the same seeded departure requests, destinations and routes. Original lane counts and default controllers form the baseline; edits form the candidate. Queued departures outside the map and unfinished trips contribute to total delay. Delay is total accumulated speed-loss time plus entry wait divided by requested trips, so it is not a completed-trip-only statistic. The live scene begins with a 75-second warmup; matched tests are separate, fresh runs.

This prototype excludes concurrent pedestrian/vehicle service, transit priority, parking maneuvers, surveyed lane connectors, measured elevations, measured turning proportions, and signal warrant analysis. Pedestrians are modeled local crossing agents, not complete sidewalk journeys. Their separately seeded demand preserves matched vehicle departure requests and routes. WALK admits new crossings; flashing clearance permits only already-started crossings to finish. Crossing width, 1.1 m/s design walking speed and a buffer set the clearance duration. Actual occupied crossings and vehicles still leaving a junction interlock the next stage. Terminal vehicle trips and departures at a junction also respect the pedestrian lockout. It is an educational game, not calibrated or validated for engineering recommendations. Satellite imagery, terrain elevation, and photogrammetric 3D tiles are **not integrated**. The real-world 3D view extrudes OSM footprints.

## Research and next steps

- [FHWA Traffic Signal Timing Manual, Chapter 5](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop08024/chapter5.htm) informs controller timing concepts.
- [OSM turn-lane tagging](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:turn:lanes) supplies directional lane semantics.
- [FHWA microsimulation guidance](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/trafficanalysistools/tat_vol3/Vol3_Guidelines.pdf) describes the need to calibrate demand and driver behavior against observed bottlenecks. This prototype remains unvalidated.
- [Chicago counts](https://data.cityofchicago.org/d/gc7y-n4xa) provide the bundled observed demand source. Outside Chicago, use a local agency's compatible CSV until other adapters are implemented.
- [Cesium content usage and attribution](https://cesium.com/learn/ion/content-usage-and-attribution-guide/) and [Google Map Tiles policies](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/policies) were reviewed for photogrammetric rendering. A future optional Cesium/3D Tiles viewer needs suitable credentials, billing configuration, dynamic provider attribution, and review of overlay/content restrictions. It should remain a visual backdrop to editable vector road geometry.
- Stronger simulation fidelity would require measured intersection turning counts, calibrated pedestrian demand, calibrated saturation flow, surveyed conflict geometry, richer lane-changing calibration, real signal plans and comparison against observed travel times. NEMA dual-ring/barrier operation is not implemented. Protected-only versus permissive left-turn operation uses a simplified sequential stage model; the pedestrian stage is an exclusive all-crosswalk interval, not concurrent parallel crossing service.

## City rendering

The city renderer generates repeating brick and limestone façades with framed windows, varied roof materials, gabled/hipped roofs, parapets, rooftop equipment, chimneys, storefront glazing, awnings and balconies. Asymmetric clusters, varied tree silhouettes, textured foliage, trunks and branches line the streets and fill mapped park areas; instanced cars have separate paint, glass, roof, wheels, lights and bumpers. Concrete sidewalks, curbs and asphalt provide street-scale contrast. Repeated details use merged geometry or instanced meshes, and moving vehicle batches avoid stale frustum bounds. Scenery rebuilds when lane widths change. Buildings stream in spatial chunks: detailed façades near the camera and lightweight mapped footprint/height meshes at distance. Spatial tree/car batches permit frustum culling on larger maps. Moving vehicles are culled against the camera before populating a 6,000-instance fleet; parked vehicles use muted paint to distinguish them. Lane permission glyphs and selected junction connectors update from controller state, and selected vehicles show a cyan route and amber destination frontage. Positive `bridge`/`layer` tags use estimated 6.2 m deck offsets with continuous adjoining road heights; underground tags can place roads under the ground surface. These offsets are illustrative, not surveyed terrain. Moving cars use brighter paint and modest zoom-dependent visual scaling for readability; physics dimensions and lane spacing remain unchanged. Warm sunlight, cool fill and footprint contact shading provide depth.

These visual details are deterministic artistic dressing, independent of the simulation. Park geometry and building footprints come from OSM; façade materials, roof forms without complete tags, tree placement and parked cars are approximations. City surface textures are created in code. The cabinet enclosure additionally bundles the unmodified CC0 Metal 032 color map from ambientCG (operated by Lennart Demes), with its source, license and hashes in the canonical credits. Original CSS geometry adds the rails, hinges, screws and hardware; no manufacturer imagery or external model is bundled.

## Live timing bench

Program keeps a sticky live output strip in the cabinet: circular R/Y/G, each approach’s protected left arrow, WALK/clearance status and phase countdown remain visible while scrolling advanced settings. Compact green-time sliders and ±5-second buttons default to Live tuning: releasing a slider or pressing a step button queues a validated program immediately. Uncheck Live tuning to stage changes and write them manually. Each row distinguishes live timing from its draft target, and highlights the currently served street. Pending edits retain safe clearance and can be replaced by a newer adjustment; the strip reports queued, paused, or active status. Advanced arrows, pedestrian timing, detection and coordination settings collapse together. Sign-controlled junctions retain their sign-only inspection.

## Cabinet traffic bench

The single shared traffic bench is anchored below the cabinet editor and stays reachable on Control, Road works and the other inspector tabs. Send selects a 10/30/60/120-car wave and mixed/left/through/right movement mix; Replay resets that wave using the current timing draft while preserving playback speed. Both open the timing editor with the pinned live signals, without switching to a separate test screen. Return restores the paused ambient district and its original playback state; works and timing edits remain. The existing Test tab and T shortcut lead to the same bench. Wave configuration locks until returning to the district.

The readout separates cars waiting on the road from requests still at entry, counts completed approach-to-exit trips, and reports simulation seconds elapsed. Tests run to full completion or 180 seconds. Completed runs compare cleared cars and mean delay against the previous completed run for that junction, mix, size and geometry; when both runs fully clear, they also report clearance-time improvement. Mid-run retiming or a pedestrian call labels comparisons exploratory. Geometry changed mid-run is excluded from baseline storage. Interrupted waves never replace the completed baseline. How this test works explains isolation, reproducibility, replay, crossing calls and score meanings in place.

## Matched original / draft traffic test

Before / After on the Traffic bench snapshots the selected wave, world geometry, other junction plans and current timing draft. Two isolated simulations use the same seed, routes, drivers, empty start and clock. The selected junction uses its original imported control/default modeled plan captured at district load for Before; After uses the captured draft. Current lanes are held constant to isolate control/timing changes. Neither run changes live traffic, saved edits, queues or playback. Both run with 0.1-second steps, no ambient demand or pedestrians, to completion or 180 seconds. Work yields periodically to keep the UI responsive.

## Web performance (v0.10)

The release uses three initial JS bundles rather than a 34-module dependency chain; esbuild preserves the shared early-location singleton across entry points and retains legal comments. Three.js source/license evidence remains in the repository while the browser downloads the minified derivation. Leaflet JavaScript and CSS load only when opening the district picker. Run `npm ci` before building; CI uses the pinned lockfile.

Roads are batched into ordinary/bridge meshes with per-edge vertex-color ranges so congestion colors remain live. Parked cars retain their modeled bodies, wheels, glazing and van geometry in three batches per scenery cell. Vehicle, foliage and bark prototypes are shared across cells and district rebuilds. Building trims are assembled directly into buffers; bounded colors/normals are packed, preserving world positions, UV precision and HDR vertex colors. Distant trees use lighter geometry with the same instance transforms and colors. Nearby trees retain full detail. Building selection work runs after meaningful camera movement, with incremental detailed chunks.

The shadow map is 2048² rather than 4096². It updates for simulation steps, camera movement, new detail/scenery and road works; repeated identical frames reuse it. Dynamic buffers upload only active entries. Hidden tabs suspend simulation/render work without changing the user's pause preference. Closed inspectors skip costly live DOM rebuilds. Matched tests snapshot the simulation graph and origins, omitting raw maps, buildings and scenery.

`node scripts/bench-render.mjs` measures deterministic CPU geometry construction, resource counts and retained typed arrays without a GPU or canvas texture generation. Results are in `benchmarks/geometry.json`; `benchmarks/javascript.json` compares source-module bytes with bundle bytes, including gzip estimates. These are not GPU VRAM or cold-network timing claims. In-browser `#world` counters report rendering submission time (CPU, not GPU elapsed time), calls, triangles, geometries, typed geometry bytes, textures and shadow size. See `benchmarks/REPORT.md` for measurement limits.

## Large-district stress tests (v0.11)

`npm run stress -- /tmp/municipal-stress-2700.json 1800 /tmp/municipal-stress-cpu.json --fetch` obtains a real 2.7 km OSM district and dated Chicago observations, then simulates 30 minutes of evening rush demand (2.5×) in 0.1-second steps and eight isolated 120-car waves. Omit `--fetch` to reuse an identical fixture. The result includes the fixture hash, graph size, step-time distribution, post-GC heap checkpoints and wave-cycle retention. Downloads stay outside the repository and release assets. Run the CPU test separately from browser smoothness measurements to avoid resource contention.

After building, copy the fixture to `dist/stress-fixture.json`, start the local server and open `http://127.0.0.1:5173/?stress=1`. The local-only Performance lab loads that fixture without saving it. Its five-minute test pans, rotates and zooms continuously, adjusts the selected signal every 15 seconds, runs three isolated 120-car waves between two rush segments, and exports frame percentile and resource checkpoints. The lab also offers a 60-second combined case: it injects 120 uniquely identified trips into the existing rush simulation while ambient demand continues, keeps the population budget, and exercises the same camera and timing edits. Normal play has no lab window. `#world` also reports a bounded rolling full-frame CPU/interval distribution, pending and upstream demand, route-cache size and optional Chromium heap telemetry. Browser heap values include GC noise and are not total process memory or GPU memory.

Long sessions retain at most 4,096 recently used routes and 36 detailed building chunks. Turn geometry and lane eligibility use graph-owned weak caches with lane-work invalidation. Indexed land-use weights replace per-departure scans of every building. Blocked entry requests reuse their lane selection and allocate a vehicle only when entry is safe. Stop/yield sign parts and pedestrian displays are batched per intersection. Distant foliage uses simpler clustered geometry; shadows concentrate their resolution around the watched area (maximum 700 m half-span), reuse fixed-sun projections during camera orbit, and refresh at most 20 times per second unless invalidated by resizing or road works. Pedestrian bodies upload only inside the camera frustum; off-camera crossing logic continues.

At saturation, at most 6,000 car requests are materialized across roads and entry buffers. Additional demand waits in per-source upstream reservoirs: counts and waiting time still enter queue/delay metrics, destinations are assigned on dispatch, and sources are serviced round-robin as capacity frees. Upstream counts appear beside the queue metric and in traffic-source data. This is an aggregate overload model, not individually routed upstream traffic; release times use the source reservoir's mean request time. Pedestrians likewise retain at most 4,000 bodies; excess requests wait in per-junction counted groups, preserve waiting-time totals, and appear in the cabinet’s waiting count. Isolated matched waves stay below these budgets and keep their exact routes and timing. See `benchmarks/STRESS.md` for results and limitations.
