# MUNICIPAL — Traffic Control Simulator

A playable browser-based WebGL traffic game with a late-1990s municipal workstation interface. JavaScript modules and locally bundled Three.js; no build dependencies, credentials, cloud account, or npm install required.

## Run

Node 20+ and Python 3 are sufficient:

```sh
cd municipal
npm run dev
```

Open http://127.0.0.1:5173. `npm run build` creates a self-contained static `dist/` for any static host. Internet is needed only for finding/importing additional districts and opening source links; the bundled Wicker Park scene and simulation work without external network requests.

## Play

Click a junction marker, signal head or sign to open a roadside controller cabinet. Its live output monitor shows red/yellow/green for each incoming compass direction, detector calls, queues and phase elapsed time. Adjust fixed-time phases or switch to detector-actuated control, and write the timing program. Protected left arrows have separate green/yellow/all-red stages; through and right-turn traffic wait while a protected arrow runs. Without protection, left-turners yield to opposing traffic on circular green. An exclusive pedestrian stage admits people on WALK, flashes DON’T WALK during calculated clearance, and holds all vehicles red until every crossing clears. The cabinet shows live arrows, walk indications, countdowns, detector calls and waiting counts. Request crossing adds a pedestrian call. Clearances are preserved. Use Road works to convert signal/stop control, add lanes, and install a left-turn bay within a $50,000 budget. Test your plan compares two seeded four-minute runs and awards the assignment at 20% lower delay with no fewer completed vehicle trips or pedestrian crossings. Click a moving car or open Vehicles to see its named origin, destination, purpose, remaining distance and route. Locate vehicle pans to it. Live lane arrows show green permission, amber yield/clearance and red hold; selecting a junction expands permitted connector paths. The traffic-load slider adjusts future arrivals (busy commute defaults to 1.50×), retaining waiting requests. Space pauses; 1/2/3/4 switch inspector tools; R runs a comparison; Enter applies a signal plan. Drag orbits, right-drag pans, and scroll zooms. PLAN switches to overhead view.

District manager supports explicit place searches through Nominatim, coordinate entry, a map preview of a 900 m, 1.8 km or 2.7 km square (0.81, 3.24 or 7.29 km²), and tiled OSM geometry imports with exact road clipping at the selected boundary. The interactive Leaflet preview lets you drag the pin, click to place it, use the current map center, enter exact coordinates, or nudge the focused pin with arrow keys. The control rectangle follows the pin; imports use that final center and selected width. ZIP-code results are only initial search locations. If a public service fails, the current district remains intact. Save plan stores the district, plans, imported counts, and road edits on the current browser; IndexedDB accommodates large map extracts; browser storage is not cross-device sync.

## Data and fidelity

The bundled Wicker Park extract includes 2,299 building footprints and a connected road graph simplified into 130 road links and 64 branching junctions within the extract boundary. Original OSM IDs, names, one-way access, and relevant tags remain in the extract. Nearby branching nodes connected within 23 m are contracted into a single control area, including the North/Damen/Milwaukee junction. A named street and its opposing approach share a phase. Mapped signal, stop and yield nodes survive degree-two road compression. Approach signs are associated with their nearby junction along the road and honor original OSM way direction; central `stop=all` means all-way stop. Signals are never invented from road class. Missing controls use explicitly labeled modeled all-way stop or priority/yield defaults; a single approach stop does not become an all-way stop. OSM supplies control type, not live signal colors or timing plans. Heights without mapped values are procedural estimates.

Chicago's Average Daily Traffic Counts (`gc7y-n4xa`) supply 80 nearby dated observations. Each link can match a same-name count within 450 m. These are dated daily counts, not live arrival rates or turning-movement measurements. Each inbound boundary gateway has a separate exponential-headway request stream. Hourly demand uses modeled shares of 8.5% morning, 9.5% evening and 5% midday, with a modeled directional share of 52% for two-way roads or 95% for one-way roads and a bound of 2,340 requested vehicles/hour per imported lane. Where no count matches, road-class daily estimates range from 1,000 for residential roads to 60,000 for motorways. Highway ramps and arterials therefore request far more traffic than neighborhood boundaries. These assumptions are exposed in the Vehicles arrival-stream list and scaled by the traffic-load setting. They are scenario inputs, not calibrated local flow predictions. CSV imports are labeled unverified.

Local departures use mapped building/amenity activity (homes, workplaces, shops, schools and leisure), bounded floor-area weights and period-specific departure frequencies. Destinations are weighted by trip purpose: morning home departures favor work and school; evening workplace trips favor homes; shopping/leisure trips use their mapped activity locations. Trips may enter, leave, pass through or remain in the district. Every valid request receives named origin/destination metadata and a directed route before admission. Local frontage points lie along public streets at least 24 m from junctions; utility roofs/sheds are omitted, and motorways/trunks never receive local driveways. Frontage points approximate access, not surveyed entrances. Incoming/outgoing gateway permissions respect one-way links. Unroutable destination pairs are resampled. Through-highway components are retained even if their ramps fall outside the district; requests stay within reachable components.

Routes minimize modeled free-flow travel time and reject movements unavailable in imported `turn:lanes` tags. Approach-specific tags retain original OSM way orientation through compression. A dedicated left bay reserves the inner lane; otherwise shared-lane defaults apply where tags are missing. Asymmetric lane geometry and turn-restriction relations are not fully modeled. Driving uses bounded acceleration/braking and an IDM-style following rule, safe adjacent-lane reservations, and explicit cubic junction connectors traversed by arc length. Cars steer continuously across the road-index boundary and enter an appropriate exit lane. Actual connector conflicts block entry; nonconflicting movements can proceed together, with following headways on a shared connector. Downstream space prevents entering a blocked box. Turn indicators and brake lights follow driving state. Vehicles finish at their assigned frontage or boundary rather than disappearing at arbitrary junctions.

Actuated controls implement minimum green, passage gap, gap-out, opposing-call maximum timers, max-out, and resting green. Protected arrows and pedestrian service retain their safety clearances. Fixed controllers support offsets; occupied junctions/crosswalks can extend clearance. Vehicle yellow is 3 s and all-red is at least 2 s. These are teaching defaults, not site-specific engineered intervals. The same movement-permission rules drive the map overlay and vehicle admission; green permission still requires conflict-free space before a car enters.

The matched comparison starts both simulations from empty at the same clock, with the same seeded departure requests, destinations and routes. Original lane counts and default controllers form the baseline; edits form the candidate. Queued departures outside the map and unfinished trips contribute to total delay. Delay is total accumulated speed-loss time plus entry wait divided by requested trips, so it is not a completed-trip-only statistic. The live scene begins with a 75-second warmup; matched tests are separate, fresh runs.

This prototype excludes concurrent pedestrian/vehicle service, transit priority, parking maneuvers, surveyed lane connectors, measured elevations, measured turning proportions, and signal warrant analysis. Pedestrians are modeled local crossing agents, not complete sidewalk journeys. Their separately seeded demand preserves matched vehicle departure requests and routes. WALK admits new crossings; flashing clearance permits only already-started crossings to finish. Crossing width, 1.1 m/s design walking speed and a buffer set the clearance duration. Actual occupied crossings and vehicles still leaving a junction interlock the next stage. Terminal vehicle trips and departures at a junction also respect the pedestrian lockout. It is an educational game, not calibrated or validated for engineering recommendations. Satellite imagery, terrain elevation, and photogrammetric 3D tiles are **not integrated**. The real-world 3D view extrudes OSM footprints.

## Research and next steps

- [FHWA Traffic Signal Timing Manual, Chapter 5](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop08024/chapter5.htm) informs controller timing concepts.
- [OSM turn-lane tagging](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:turn:lanes) supplies directional lane semantics.
- [FHWA microsimulation guidance](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/trafficanalysistools/tat_vol3/Vol3_Guidelines.pdf) describes the need to calibrate demand and driver behavior against observed bottlenecks. This prototype remains unvalidated.
- [Chicago counts](https://data.cityofchicago.org/d/gc7y-n4xa) provide the bundled observed demand source. Outside Chicago, use a local agency's compatible CSV until other adapters are implemented.
- [Cesium content usage and attribution](https://cesium.com/learn/ion/content-usage-and-attribution-guide/) and [Google Map Tiles policies](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/policies) were reviewed for photogrammetric rendering. A future optional Cesium/3D Tiles viewer needs suitable credentials, billing configuration, dynamic provider attribution, and review of overlay/content restrictions. It should remain a visual backdrop to editable vector road geometry.
- Stronger simulation fidelity would require measured intersection turning counts, calibrated pedestrian demand, calibrated saturation flow, surveyed conflict geometry, richer lane-changing calibration, real signal plans and comparison against observed travel times. NEMA dual-ring/barrier operation is not implemented. Protected-only versus permissive left-turn operation uses a simplified sequential stage model; the pedestrian stage is an exclusive all-crosswalk interval, not concurrent parallel crossing service.

```sh
npm run build
```

Tests cover phase safety, actuated detection and opposing-call timers, coordination offsets, safe plan transitions, one-way routing, real-map contraction, identical baseline scores, matched seeded demand after edits, finite vehicle states, accounting for unfinished demand, and clearing a stop junction without repeated stopping, directional control mapping, safe installation from priority control, district tiling, exact boundary clipping, turn classification, protected-turn permissions, pedestrian entry/clearance interlocks, pending edits during a crossing, moved-pin boundaries, highway-versus-residential rates, one-way source/sink roles, frontage routing, lane restrictions, continuous turn position/heading, connector conflicts, lane-change gaps, source reproducibility after edits and retained highway components.

`credits.json` is the canonical current/historical asset list. The in-game About screen reads it directly. License/provenance evidence is in `licenses/`; the credits check validates all data/vendor files and SHA-256 hashes. Keep historical entries when replacing an asset. Original procedural visuals need no external assets. Chicago's standalone license/version is unspecified in retrieved metadata; portal terms and the unresolved fact are retained in provenance rather than invented.

## City rendering

The city renderer generates repeating brick and limestone façades with framed windows, varied roof materials, gabled/hipped roofs, parapets, rooftop equipment, chimneys, storefront glazing, awnings and balconies. Asymmetric clusters, varied tree silhouettes, textured foliage, trunks and branches line the streets and fill mapped park areas; instanced cars have separate paint, glass, roof, wheels, lights and bumpers. Concrete sidewalks, curbs and asphalt provide street-scale contrast. Repeated details use merged geometry or instanced meshes, and moving vehicle batches avoid stale frustum bounds. Scenery rebuilds when lane widths change. Buildings stream in spatial chunks: detailed façades near the camera and lightweight mapped footprint/height meshes at distance. Spatial tree/car batches permit frustum culling on larger maps. Moving vehicles are culled against the camera before populating a 6,000-instance fleet; parked vehicles use muted paint to distinguish them. Lane permission glyphs and selected junction connectors update from controller state, and selected vehicles show a cyan route and amber destination frontage. Positive `bridge`/`layer` tags use estimated 6.2 m deck offsets with continuous adjoining road heights; underground tags can place roads under the ground surface. These offsets are illustrative, not surveyed terrain. Moving cars use brighter paint and modest zoom-dependent visual scaling for readability; physics dimensions and lane spacing remain unchanged. Warm sunlight, cool fill and footprint contact shading provide depth.

These visual details are deterministic artistic dressing, independent of the simulation. Park geometry and building footprints come from OSM; façade materials, roof forms without complete tags, tree placement and parked cars are approximations. All surface textures are created in code, with no external texture downloads.
